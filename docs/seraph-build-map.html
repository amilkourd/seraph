Briefing on the way

2

Every occupant on the line

The incident gets its own Gabriel line. 112 calls for this address are routed to it, and Gabriel calls every occupant listed in the building manager's software. One agent per call, all in parallel, in the caller's language. Each call asks where they are, how many people, smoke, mobility, injuries.

Rosetta
occupants.list for the numbers, dispatch.write_112 for each call, positions from plan.read

Seraph never decides whether people stay or leave. The commander decides on the radio and Gabriel passes it on. At Grenfell, lifting « stay put » too late cost lives.

Phone A

Halo views

How a view works

  • Nothing is prebuilt. Seraph decides what the firefighter needs right now and asks Silex for that view.
  • Devin builds it from the UI kit, the live data and Rosetta's screenshots, used as visual references: the facade from Earth, the photo of the plan.
  • Each view is a simple animated scheme, not raw data: a block, a street, a staircase, an arrow. It can be interactive: tap a floor, a door, a team.
  • Holo points at things on the screenshots. Seraph computes positions and the route, and the view only displays them, so no model guesses the arrow.
  • The view is wired to the live state and moves on its own. Gabriel says the same thing on the radio at the same moment. Every element shows its source.

The right thing at the right moment

  • Dispatch: Silex starts the approach view with Earth and Street View screenshots. The drive is its build window.
  • Arrival: block, street, truck, the arrow to the door.
  • Plan read: the inside route, smoke shaded, the arrow along it.
  • Calls coming in: who is where, floor by floor.
  • « Binôme engagé »: team tracking with exit times.
  • Anything the commander asks: a new view, built on request.

If a view isn't ready in time, the facts show as a plain list on Halo, labelled, until Devin delivers.
